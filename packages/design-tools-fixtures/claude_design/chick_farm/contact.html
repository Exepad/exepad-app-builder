FAQ

Things people ask, ranked by frequency.

If your question isn't here, the email is up there ↑.

Are you certified organic?+
No, but functionally yes. Certification costs $1,500/year and a lot of paperwork, and it doesn't change how we farm. Our feed is certified organic from the mill in Brattleboro. Our pasture has been chemical-free since we bought it. We've decided to put that money into hens instead.
Do you ship eggs?+
No. We tried it for one summer in 2022 and the breakage rate was about 8%, which felt awful — both for the eggs and for what we were charging people. We deliver wholesale within 25 miles. Beyond that, find us at a stockist.
Why are the eggs different sizes?+
Because real hens lay different-sized eggs. Pullets (first-year layers) lay smaller. Older hens lay bigger. Heritage breeds vary more than commercial hybrids. We hand-grade so you'll get a reasonable mix in any carton, but a uniform dozen is a sign that machines, not chickens, are involved somewhere.
Do I need to refrigerate?+
Not strictly, for the first 2–3 weeks. We don't wash our eggs, so the natural cuticle is intact and they're shelf-stable on the counter for a while — that's how the rest of the world stores them. After 3 weeks, fridge them. They'll keep another 8 weeks easily.
Can my kid hold a chicken?+
Yes, with our help, on Saturdays. Brunhilde is the designated holding hen — calm, heavy, completely unbothered. We have hand-wash stations near the coops. (And we're sticklers about washing afterward — chickens are great, salmonella is not.)
What does "pasture-raised" actually mean?+
In the U.S., the term is unregulated, which is a problem. Marketers use it to mean anything from "108 sq ft per bird" to "a small door to a concrete patio." For us it means: every hen, every daylight hour, on real grass, on rotating paddocks. About 760 sq ft per bird, which is a lot.
Are eggs really worth $8?+
A grocery dozen is $4–6. Ours is $8. The difference is feed (organic, soy-free is roughly 2.5x conventional), labor (we hand-collect twice daily), land (paddock rotation needs space), and the fact that our hens lay 220/year, not 320. We're not getting rich. The math works because Marta loves it.
Can I bring my dog?+
No, sorry. Even the friendliest dogs make hens nervous, and a nervous hen lays fewer eggs for a day or two. We have a shaded spot with water in the parking area for dogs to wait in cooler months. In summer please leave them home.
Do you sell live chickens or chicks?+
Not regularly. Once or twice a year we have surplus pullets to rehome — usually announced by email to people who've asked. Email Marta if you want to be on that list.
